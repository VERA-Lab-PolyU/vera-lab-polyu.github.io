# Academic AI/ML Lab Naming Landscape

> **Decision update — 2026-10-07:** The group adopted **VERA Lab** with the descriptor **Trustworthy & Collaborative Intelligence**. VERA is treated as a proper name, not a mandatory acronym. The analysis below is retained as the research record that preceded the decision.

_Prepared 3 September 2026_

## Purpose and scope

This scan examines how academic AI/ML groups name themselves across six areas relevant to Prof. Yang Liu's group at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University (PolyU): federated/distributed learning; privacy/trustworthy/responsible AI; collaborative and multi-agent AI; healthcare/biomedical AI; AI for science/discovery; and broad PI-led ML groups. The target group's verified public scope includes stream machine learning, federated learning, trustworthy AI, statistical mechanics, and industrial applications; current PolyU material also connects Prof. Liu's work to federated foundation models and collaborative agents. PolyU's Research Institute for Federated Learning (RIFL), where Prof. Liu is Co-Director, already owns the institution-level “federated learning” identity and covers privacy, security, collaborative intelligence, health, and industrial applications ([PolyU profile](https://www.polyu.edu.hk/riaiot/people/riaiot-people/prof-yang-liu/?sc_lang=en); [PolyU job specification](https://jobs.polyu.edu.hk/job_detail.php?job=260423002); [RIFL announcement](https://www.polyu.edu.hk/paai/news-and-events/news/2026/20260401-establishment-of-rifl/)).

The evidence base is 36 official university, department, institute, or lab-controlled pages. Scope summaries below are conservative paraphrases of those pages. This is a naming-landscape scan, not a global trademark or domain clearance.

## Naming patterns and implications

| Pattern | What the sample shows | Fit for this group |
|---|---|---|
| **PI name + Lab/Research Group** | Luo Lab, Noble Research Lab, YODA Lab | Most durable and organizationally accurate. It survives topic changes and distinguishes the group from RIFL. The tradeoff is weaker standalone thematic branding. |
| **Distinctive brand + explanatory descriptor** | ARGUS, SymbioticLab, HPC Forge | High memorability and searchability; scope lives in a tagline. It requires careful semantic and legal/domain clearance and should not be reverse-engineered into a strained acronym. |
| **Stable mechanism + Lab** | FOCAL, MAS, DAIR, SciML | Scientifically legible when one mechanism truly organizes the work. For this group, “collaborative” alone is not precise enough; “federated” is too narrow and already institutionally occupied. |
| **Values adjective + AI Lab** | Multiple exact “Trustworthy AI Lab” instances | Immediately clear but crowded and weakly differentiating. Better as a descriptor or research pillar. |
| **Application domain + AI/Intelligence** | HAIL, UCLA Biomedical AI, Matter Lab | Strong when a domain defines the group. Healthcare and science are important applications here, but neither alone covers the full portfolio. |
| **Enumerated phrase engineered into acronym** | SPEED, AICMSH, STAIR, CAMP | Can work when the expansion reads naturally. Long lists age badly and can make every future topic change a naming problem. |

### Crowded terms

- **“Trustworthy AI Lab” is demonstrably crowded**, appearing as an exact name at Harvard, UCLA, and elsewhere in the sample; TAIL and STAIR are close variants.
- **“Machine Learning Lab,” “Artificial Intelligence Lab,” and “Machine Intelligence Lab” are generic category labels.** They are durable but provide poor differentiation without an institution or PI name.
- **“Collaborative AI Lab” is already an exact institutional name at Bocconi University**, whose official page uses the acronym CAIL ([Bocconi](https://www.sdabocconi.it/en/faculty-research/research/collaborative-ai-lab/)). Purdue's CAMP Lab also owns a strong adjacent formulation.
- **“Scientific Machine Learning/SciML” is an established cross-institutional field label**, not a unique group identity, as the Texas A&M and Brown examples show.
- **“AI in Medicine,” “Biomedical AI,” and “Health Intelligence” are active application-name families.** They communicate health orientation but would overstate the role of one application in the target group.

### Ambiguous terms

- **Collaborative intelligence** can refer to multi-party ML, multi-agent cooperation, human–AI teamwork, educational collaboration, or organizational collective intelligence. The ambiguity is observable in official uses ranging from NYU Abu Dhabi's graph/RL work to the University of North Florida's student–AI partnership lab ([NYU Abu Dhabi](https://nyuad.nyu.edu/en/research/researchers/research-staff/samyam-lamichhane.html); [UNF](https://www.unf.edu/silverfield/loci/tech/UNITE-Lab.html)).
- **AI systems** can mean ML infrastructure, deployed socio-technical systems, autonomous agents, or simply multiple AI methods. It is flexible but must be anchored by a precise tagline.
- **Discovery** may mean scientific discovery, drug/material discovery, information discovery, or exploratory analytics. It should be qualified if used in the formal name.
- **Responsible** often signals governance, policy, ethics, and social impact; **trustworthy** may also include robustness, privacy, security, and measurement. Neither word alone identifies the group's central technical mechanism.
- **Federated intelligence** is broader-sounding than federated learning but remains tightly associated with RIFL and can imply an institute-level umbrella rather than a PI group.

## Collision risks for the current candidate family

1. **Collaborative AI Systems Lab / CAIS Lab — high acronym risk, moderate phrase risk.** The exact expanded phrase did not surface as a prominent academic lab in this targeted scan, but **CAIS Lab** is already used by Penn State's Control and Automation for Intelligent Systems Lab ([Penn State CAIS](https://www.caislab.com/contact/)). “Collaborative AI” is also already occupied by Bocconi. The acronym should therefore be treated as unavailable or weak.
2. **Collaborative AI Lab / CAIL — high risk.** It is the exact name and acronym of Bocconi's lab; CAIL is also used for Causal AI Lab at UMBC and CUNY AI Lab ([UMBC](https://ai.umbc.edu/labs-groups/); [CUNY](https://ailab.gc.cuny.edu/about/)).
3. **Collaborative Intelligence Lab — high semantic and identity risk.** Exact and near-exact academic uses already span technical AI and human/organizational collaboration; audiences cannot infer federated/model/agent collaboration from the name alone.
4. **Trustworthy Collaborative AI Lab — moderate-to-high category risk.** It combines two crowded/ambiguous phrases and resembles existing trustworthy and collaborative AI brands. It is descriptive but not readily ownable.
5. **Federated Learning/Intelligence Lab — high institutional-confusion risk.** PolyU already has RIFL, with Prof. Liu in formal leadership. A PI lab using the same organizing term could look like a duplicate, subunit, or unofficial alternate brand.
6. **Healthcare AI or AI for Science Lab — high scope risk.** Either would incorrectly elevate one application family over the group's shared methodological core.

## Recommendation: separate ownership from research positioning

The strongest architecture is a **stable PI-owned name plus a precise, revisable descriptor**, rather than forcing every method and application into one acronym:

> **Yang Liu Lab**  
> *Collaborative and trustworthy learning across data, models, agents, and domains.*

or, in a slightly more formal academic register:

> **Yang Liu Research Group**  
> *Federated learning, collaborative AI, trustworthy foundation models and agents, with applications in health, science, and industry.*

This structure has four advantages:

1. It makes ownership unambiguous: this is Prof. Liu's PI-led group, not the official RIFL site or a jointly led institute.
2. It preserves “federated,” “collaborative,” “trustworthy,” healthcare, and scientific applications as visible research content without freezing them into the legal/brand name.
3. It remains accurate if the balance shifts among FL, foundation models, agents, healthcare, and AI for science.
4. It avoids the clearest observed collisions: Trustworthy AI Lab, Collaborative AI Lab/CAIL, CAIS Lab, and SciML Lab.

If a thematic brand is still desired, the evidence supports a **two-level identity**:

> **[Distinctive brand] — the Yang Liu Lab at PolyU**  
> *Collaborative and trustworthy AI across data, models, agents, and domains.*

The distinctive word should be chosen only after checking university naming policy, exact web results, scholarly organizations, GitHub handles, domains, and relevant trademarks. It should evoke the mechanism—distributed contributions becoming stronger collective intelligence—without using “federated,” “health,” or “science” as the sole identity. The sample favors a short real word or natural acronym over a long expansion assembled from all current topics.

## Practical naming criteria for the next iteration

Score future candidates against these six questions before aesthetic preference:

1. **Ownership:** Does the name clearly denote Prof. Liu's group rather than RIFL, PAAI, or a co-led PolyU unit?
2. **Mechanism:** Does it suggest learning or intelligence assembled across data, models, agents, or organizations?
3. **Breadth:** Can healthcare, science, and industry all appear as first-class applications without stretching the name?
4. **Durability:** Would it remain accurate if “foundation models” or “agents” were replaced by the next technical paradigm?
5. **Distinctiveness:** Is the exact phrase and acronym separable from existing academic labs in search and conversation?
6. **Naturalness:** Does the full name read as ordinary academic English without filler words introduced only to complete an acronym?

On present evidence, **Yang Liu Lab / Yang Liu Research Group with a strong descriptor is the defensible default**. A branded alternative can outperform it only if it passes a dedicated collision and institutional-clearance round; the previously proposed **CAIS Lab** should not be the default because its acronym is already in academic use and its leading phrase sits in a crowded “Collaborative AI” family.
